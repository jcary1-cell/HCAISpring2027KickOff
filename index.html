Teaching and Learning in Human-Centered AI: A Shared Vision

Goal: leave with a draft of 4–6 shared pillars for HCAI courses, and a clear line between what's shared across the program and what stays with each faculty member. Doing this first, while energy is high, means everything after it has something to point back to. Clear a long wall and tape up butcher paper before people arrive.

  1. Frame it 5 min

    Shared pillars give students a coherent program. They don't mean everyone teaches the same way. Introduce tight and loose: a few things we hold tight in every HCAI course, and plenty we keep loose so each person teaches their own way.

  2. Silent brainstorm 8 min

    Sticky notes, one idea per note, five to seven each. Writing silently first means the loudest voice doesn't set the agenda before everyone has thought.

    By the time a student finishes the HCAI program, they should be able to… care about… have experienced…

  3. Cluster it 12 min

    • Everyone puts their notes on the wall. No explanation needed.
    • Silent clustering, 5 min: anyone can move any note to group similar ideas. If two people keep moving the same note, make a copy.
    • Then talk, 7 min: name each cluster on a bigger card. These are the candidate pillars.

  4. Dot vote 5 min

    Each person gets three dots in their spirit color. Stack them however you like on the clusters that matter most program-wide. Circle the top 4–6. That's the draft.

  5. Tight / Loose wall 20 min

    Split into 2–3 small groups, each taking 1–2 pillars. Groups write concrete examples on sticky notes and place them in one of three columns. Example for a pillar like "Ethics and impact":

    Every HCAI course does thisStudents examine who's affected by an AI system
    Every course touches this, its own waySome kind of reflection on responsible use
    Your course, your callWhich frameworks, case studies, or tools you use

    One-minute report back from each group. Disagreements are useful. Mark them with a "?" and keep moving. They become topics for the semester check-ins.

  6. Course map 5 min

    A poster with the final pillars across the top and each person's course down the side. Everyone places a spirit-color dot wherever their course hits a pillar. You'll see the program's strengths and gaps in five minutes. Leave every wall up. Lunch and the Canvas hour both build on it.

Working lunch: Food for Thought

A grab-and-go spread from Chartwells: sandwiches, cookies, and coffee. Relaxed, with just enough structure that conversation goes past small talk.

Table setup

  • Food table off to the side so the seating tables stay clear for paper and markers. Cookies and coffee can sit at the far end, which gives people a reason to get up again later.
  • Butcher paper over the tables with markers. People doodle and jot as they talk. Photograph it afterward. It's often the most honest record of what people care about.
  • A conversation menu at each table, laid out like a restaurant menu.
  • A few of the big AI questions from the intros, written on cards and dropped on each table.
Appetizers

Easy openers

  • What's a piece of tech you've had a love/hate relationship with?
  • What's the best class you ever took, and what made it great?
  • What did you think AI would look like when you were a kid?
Entrées

The meatier stuff

  • What do your students already know about AI that you don't?
  • Where have you seen AI go wrong in your field? What would a human-centered fix have looked like?
  • One thing you'd never let AI do in your classroom, and one thing you'd love it to do?
  • Which pillar from this morning are you most excited to teach? Which one makes you a little nervous?
  • What does "human-centered" mean to you, in one sentence?
Dessert

Looking ahead

  • If this program is wildly successful in five years, what are our graduates doing?
  • Who in this room would you like to collaborate with, and on what?

The cookie-and-coffee swap about 1:10

Announce cookies and a coffee refill, and ask everyone to come back to a seat next to someone they haven't talked to yet. It takes 30 seconds and doubles the real conversations. It also wakes everyone up before the Canvas hour.

Blueprint scavenger hunt 20 min · pairs

Pair people from different departments. Each pair gets a half-sheet checklist and stars anything confusing while CTL circulates. Sample items:

  • One place a pillar from this morning already shows up
  • One locked item and one item you can edit
  • The HCAI AI-use / syllabus statement
  • Where to request a change to the blueprint
  • The accessibility checker
  • One resource you didn't know existed and would actually use

"Still Wondering" parking lot

The poster that's been up since people walked in. CTL answers the Canvas questions in their last 10 minutes, and anything left over gets an owner during the wrap-up.

Worth covering if there's time

  • How the blueprint gets updated mid-semester, and how faculty hear about changes
  • A short "week before classes" checklist
  • Who to contact for what: CTL, Justin, or IT

Logistics + close

Semester check-ins 4 min

A pre-printed poster with 4–5 options, like "Every other Tuesday, 12–12:45, Zoom." Everyone dots what works. Pick the winner on the spot if it's clear, or follow up by email.

Communication pathways 3 min

Show of hands on where people actually want to talk: a Teams channel, an email list, or a Canvas Hub discussion. Then go back to the "Still Wondering" board and give each open item an owner and a follow-up date.

Exit card 3 min

On an index card: one word for how you feel about the semester, and one thing you'll try in your course. Collect at the door. Then a group photo in front of the pillar wall.
